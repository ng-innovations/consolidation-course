Layer 4 — Overview

Industry-Specific Handling

Coming soon

Layer 4 is a planned section of the course. No lessons are published yet. This page describes the intent.

Layer 4 collects consolidation handling that's specific to particular industries. Where Layer 3 covers edge cases that can appear in any group, Layer 4 covers situations that only arise (or only matter) in certain industry contexts.

Someone could safely skip Layer 4 entirely on a first read. It's there for the moment you find yourself working on a feature that touches an industry-specific scenario, or auditing a customer who operates in one of these spaces.

What goes here

Topics likely to land in Layer 4 include:

  • REITs — consolidation of property-holding SPVs, joint-venture treatment of co-investment vehicles, the VIE analysis that often applies
  • Private equity fund-of-funds — multi-tier ownership through fund structures, fair-value vs. consolidation decisions, NAV-based reporting
  • Insurance companies — statutory accounting overlays, separate-account consolidation rules, reinsurance treatment
  • Banks and financial institutions — consolidation of structured entities, regulatory consolidation vs. accounting consolidation, capital ratio implications
  • Healthcare systems — non-profit consolidation rules, joint operating agreements between providers
  • Government and non-profit entities — fund accounting overlays, GASB and FASB ASC 958 differences from corporate consolidation
  • Investment companies under ASC 946 — the consolidation exemption and its limits

The list will grow as the team encounters specific customer scenarios that need to be documented.

Who this layer is for

Layer 4 is reference material for developers and analysts working on features that touch a specific industry. Most readers won't need most of it. The structure is designed so that each industry's lessons can be read in isolation — no required reading order across industries.

How Layer 4 differs from Layer 3

Layer 3 (Advanced Concepts) covers complexities that can appear in any group, regardless of industry. Layer 4 covers complexities that are tied to a particular industry's structure or regulatory environment. The heuristic: if a wrinkle could apply to an SaaS company, a manufacturer, a retailer, and a REIT, it's Layer 3. If it only really matters for one of them, it's Layer 4.
