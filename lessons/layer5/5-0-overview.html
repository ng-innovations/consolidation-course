Layer 5 — Overview

Coming from Other Systems

Coming soon

Layer 5 is a planned section of the course. No lessons are published yet. This page describes the intent.

Layer 5 is mental-model translation for developers and analysts who've worked with other consolidation systems. It's not a study of those systems, and it's not competitive analysis. It's a translation layer: if you've internalized how NetSuite OneWorld handles eliminations, or how Oracle EPM thinks about FX translation, or how Workday Adaptive sets up entity hierarchies, those mental models will follow you into our product whether or not we want them to. Layer 5 makes the translation explicit.

The framing for each lesson is "if you're coming from X, here's how the concepts you already know map to ours, and where the workflows differ."

What goes here

Topics likely to land in Layer 5 include:

  • Coming from NetSuite OneWorld — how subsidiary setup, elimination subsidiaries, and consolidation rate types map to our model
  • Coming from Oracle EPM (formerly HFM) — how dimensional structures, account properties, and consolidation rules translate
  • Coming from Workday Adaptive — how the modeling vs. consolidation distinction lines up with our pipeline
  • Coming from SAP Group Reporting (or BPC) — how their data models compare
  • Coming from Excel-based consolidation — for readers whose mental model is workbooks, tabs, and formulas

Each lesson is intentionally short. It's not teaching you the source system — you already know that. It's mapping vocabulary, flagging differences in workflow, and pointing back to the relevant Layer 2 lessons for our approach.

Who this layer is for

Layer 5 is for readers who have hands-on experience with at least one other consolidation system and find themselves wanting to translate. It's optional for readers without that background — nothing in Layer 5 introduces concepts that aren't already covered in earlier layers.

This layer is also useful in customer-facing contexts: when supporting a customer migrating from one of these systems, the relevant Layer 5 lesson can help frame conversations about how their existing process maps to our product.

What this layer is not

  • Not a tutorial on how to use the source systems
  • Not a competitive comparison or feature matrix
  • Not a marketing document — the goal is honest translation, including where our approach is different (and saying why)
  • Not exhaustive — small differences in UI labels or terminology are out of scope; only workflow-level concepts are mapped
