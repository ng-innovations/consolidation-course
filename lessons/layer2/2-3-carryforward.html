Carryforward & opening balances

Financial periods don't exist in isolation. Each period's opening balance is the prior period's closing balance, and the math has to work out at every transition. This lesson covers carryforward — the mechanics of rolling balances between periods.

Pipeline reference: Carryforward shows up in two places — Validate Carryforward (step C.6, at the start of each period's consolidation) and Balance Carryforward (step D.14, at the end of the period to prepare for the next one).

Balance sheet vs. income statement behavior

Carryforward works differently for the two main account types.

Balance sheet accounts roll forward

An asset, liability, or equity account's closing balance at the end of one period is the opening balance of the next period. The cash you had on December 31 is the cash you have on January 1.

This is true regardless of period frequency. Monthly closes work the same way — January closing cash becomes February opening cash.

Income statement accounts reset to zero — but go somewhere first

Revenue and expense accounts measure activity during a period. At the end of the fiscal year, they need to start fresh for the new year — January's revenue shouldn't include December's.

But the profit (or loss) for the year doesn't just disappear. It moves into retained earnings on the balance sheet. This is called the year-end "closing entry."

So at year end:

  • Revenue and expense accounts close to zero (and stay at zero on Jan 1 of the new year).
  • Net income for the year is added to retained earnings (whose closing balance carries forward as the opening balance for the new year).

Mid-year monthly closes

For monthly closes within a fiscal year, the income statement accounts don't reset. They accumulate year-to-date. So at the end of Q1, the revenue account reads three months of revenue, and at the end of Q2, it reads six months.

This is why income statement accounts have an opening balance of zero only at the start of each fiscal year — but during the year, their "opening" balance is the prior period's closing balance, just like balance sheet accounts.

Why carryforward validation matters

It seems obvious that this period's opening should match last period's closing. But in practice, it's frequently broken — and broken carryforward is one of the most common sources of consolidation errors.

How it breaks

  • Late entries. Someone posted an adjustment to last period after that period was closed (but before it was locked). The closing balance changed, but the opening of the new period wasn't updated.
  • Restatements. A prior period's numbers had to be corrected. The new opening balances need to reflect the correction.
  • Mapping changes. A local account that mapped to group account 1110 last period now maps to 1120. Without careful handling, the balance disappears from 1110 and appears in 1120 with no historical context.
  • Currency translation. A subsidiary's translated opening balance depends on the historical FX rate. If the rate used for translation has changed (or been corrected), opening balances move.
  • Acquisition/divestiture. A new subsidiary's opening balances need to be brought in. A divested one's need to be removed.

What the carryforward check does

For every balance sheet account in every entity, the system compares:

Any non-zero difference has to be investigated. Sometimes there's a legitimate reason (a posted prior-period adjustment); sometimes it's a real error to be fixed before the new period can proceed.

Year-end carryforward steps

At fiscal year-end, the carryforward operation has more work to do than a normal period transition:

  1. Calculate net income. Sum the income statement accounts.
  2. Post the closing entry. Move net income into retained earnings.
  3. Reset income statement accounts to zero. They start fresh.
  4. Carry forward balance sheet balances. Closing → next period's opening.
  5. Carry forward equity components. CTA cumulative balance, other OCI components, accumulated NCI, etc.
  6. Update beginning-of-year ownership percentages. If any acquisitions/divestitures happened, lock in the new structure.

Equity components carryforward

Equity has multiple components, and each needs to be carried forward correctly:

ComponentHow it carries forward
Share capitalClosing balance carries forward. Only changes via share issuance/buyback.
Retained earningsClosing balance + net income for new period − dividends paid.
CTACumulative — carries forward. New period's translation adds to it.
NCIClosing balance + NCI's share of new period's income − NCI dividends.
Other OCICumulative — carries forward like CTA.

Where this happens in the pipeline

Carryforward appears at both ends of the period cycle:

  • At the start (step C.6): Validate that opening balances match prior closing. Reject the period if they don't (or surface for resolution).
  • At the end (step D.14): After the period is locked, roll closing balances forward into the next period's opening, and apply year-end closing entries if it's year-end.

State transitions

Carryforward operations span across periods. The closing snapshot of Period N (Top state) becomes part of the input that produces Period N+1's Local state. Specifically, opening balances are part of the input data; the validation just confirms they match what was locked previously.

Why this matters for the software

For developers

Carryforward is where periods touch each other. A few design points:

  • Locked periods must be immutable. Once Period N is locked, its closing balances cannot change — otherwise Period N+1's opening becomes a moving target. Authorized "unlock" should require explicit action and audit logging.
  • Opening balance is its own concept, not the same row as closing balance. Store and surface them separately. A common bug pattern: showing only "current period balance" makes it hard to debug carryforward issues.
  • Year-end closing entries should be deterministic and replayable. The math is mechanical; don't let it become a human-driven process.
  • Don't conflate accounting carryforward with database carryforward. Database rows from last period don't need to be "moved" — they stay where they are. The system just needs to know that Period N+1's opening balance for account X is whatever Period N's closing balance for account X was.

Summary

  • Balance sheet account closing balances become next period's opening balances.
  • Income statement accounts reset to zero at fiscal year-end, with net income closing to retained earnings.
  • The carryforward validation check ensures this period's opening balances match last period's closing.
  • Mismatches arise from late entries, restatements, mapping changes, FX rate changes, and acquisitions.
  • Equity components — share capital, RE, CTA, NCI, OCI — each carry forward with their own rules.
  • Carryforward happens at both ends of the period cycle: validate at start, post-close at end.
